- **The whole start: `STATE/probes/bench_shelf_covers.py`.** From the checkout: `BOOKS=2000 RUNS=3 BOOKS_DIR=/tmp/mcomix-loop/benchbooks env -u WAYLAND_DISPLAY GDK_BACKEND=x11 xvfb-run -d -s "-screen 0 1280x1024x24" timeout -k 5 280 python3 <probe> > SCRATCH/bench.txt 2>&1; grep -E "^(cold|warm|stored|[0-9]+ books)" SCRATCH/bench.txt`. It builds BOOKS zips once in BOOKS_DIR (one 1000x1500 JPEG and 19 empty members each; keep the directory on the tmpfs between runs, 2000 take a minute to build), adds them to a library on `MComixTest`, and starts a `MainWindow()` with no book: once cold (no cover stored), then RUNS times warm (covers stored, the memory cache emptied, which is a program start). One line per start, milliseconds from just before `MainWindow()`: window built, `display_covers()` called, first cover handed to its cell, last cover of a cell in view, last cover asked for; then how many cells are in view, bound and made, and the mean wall time of `_get_pixbuf` on its worker. `TIMELINE=1` adds the ask batches and the order covers were handed over in; `PROFILE=1` profiles the main thread up to the first ask; `COVER=250` sets the cover size; `DROP_CACHE=1` drops the stored covers from the page cache first (no effect on a tmpfs).
- **One cover alone: `STATE/probes/prof_cover.py`**, same wrapper and BOOKS_DIR. Prints the cost of one warm cover on one thread and of a memory-cache hit, then a profile of 200 covers. Filter the output: `grep -v "DEBUG\|INFO\|MESA\|Deprecat"`.

- **What the lines mean.** The grid binds about 32 rows of cells whatever is in view: 449 cells for 78 covers in view at 1200x900, and all of them are asked for in one batch from an idle, about 25 ms after `display_covers()`. `update_shelf()` itself runs from an idle after the first frame (`main.py`, `GLib.idle_add(self.update_shelf)`), which is why `display_covers()` comes 25 ms after the window is built. Under 24 workers the mean wall time per cover stays 4-10 ms although one cover costs 0.05 ms on one thread: that is the interpreter lock and the hand-over to the main thread, not work. The sorter's compare runs once per book for a collection already in order.
- **The memory cache is the process's.** `pixbuf_cache.get_pixbuf_cache()` outlives a test, and `MComixTest` does not empty it: a test that needs a cover drawn from disk empties it before its window is built, or another test's cover of the same file is shown and nothing is read or kept.
- **A file format for small pictures**: for 83x125 RGB, a level 1 PNG takes 0.60 ms to write and 0.23 ms to read at 30.0 kB, the raw pixels 0.05 ms and 0.004 ms at 31.1 kB; for 167x250, 2.36 and 1.08 ms against 0.05 and 0.008 ms. Decoding dominates at every size the library draws, which is why the kept covers are raw pixels (`Thumbnailer.sized()`).
- **A scroll jump: `JUMP=0.5`** on the same probe scrolls to that share of the grid in one step once the start has settled and prints a second line: first and last cover in view, settled, how many were made and how many of those for cells no longer bound. `JUMP_EARLY=1` scrolls as soon as the first covers are asked for, the others in flight. With `TIMELINE=1` it adds when the workers made the covers against when the cells got them, the cells' positions, and the order handed over. Numbers at 5936e256, 2000 books: cold, settled, first in view 75-133 ms, last in view 479-495 (418 and 779 at ea81b093); cold, early, last in view 887-928 with 543-552 covers made (2750 with 898); warm, settled, 6-9 and 21-23 (21-28 and 35-89); warm, early, 170-328 either way.
- **A wait that looks at the cells starves the main loop.** `wait_for()` with a predicate that calls into GTK for each cell (450 `compute_bounds()`, twice a turn) held every cover back until all 24 workers were done: each PyGObject call gives the interpreter lock up and waits its turn for it again. Workers made the first cover at 66 ms, the cells got it at 2067. Wait on Python state only (`item.thumbnail`, `covers._bound`), and read the cells once afterwards. The same holds for the program: under 24 busy workers the main thread's rebinding after a jump takes 150-300 ms where it takes under 10 idle.
- **In view means mapped.** GTK keeps cells bound off screen, and their bounds are stale ones inside the viewport: test `cell.get_mapped()` as well as the bounds, or 141 cells count as in view where 78 are. The first entry stays bound wherever the grid is scrolled to (the focus), so a test of an entry going off screen takes another one.
- **A collection drawn again: `SWITCH=N`.** The same probe with `SWITCH=4` redraws the shown collection four times after the start and prints, for each, display_covers' time, the time until no cover is pending, the first paint with how many bound cells were still blank, and the covers the worker made since. At 3e620df5, warm, 2000 books: display_covers 58-59 ms, settled 58-59, first paint 66-68 with 0 of 449 blank, 0 made; before it 43-49 ms first paint with 449 of 449 blank and 449 made. `PROFILE=1` profiles switch 0: 449 `_setup_cell` (about 10 ms in `_ThumbnailCell.__init__`) shows the grid builds its cells anew over a full splice.
